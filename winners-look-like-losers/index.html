Research note · Nasdaq intraday momentum · November 2026

Winners and losers look identical at entry

What I built, where I'm stuck, and the help I'm looking for.

Vivaan Desai · 17, still in school · I built and run this system myself on a demo account, and publish the results that didn't work too. PDF version

The system

An automated trading system for the Nasdaq 100 index (CFD, 15-minute bars). It joins moves that are already under way, cuts losing trades quickly and lets winning trades run. Every trade risks the same small fraction of the account. It has traded in real time on a demo account since July 2026.

Where it stands

The backtest shows a small, steady edge: +205R over 5,338 trades in 2022–26, about +1R a week. Live (demo) is roughly flat: −4.5R since July, +1.1R since the current exits went in on 12 October. R is the amount risked on one trade.

Where I'm stuck

The edge comes from a few trades that run far. I can't tell those trades apart from losers in advance, and five different kinds of test say the data I have can't do it. Most weeks end close to flat, and I don't yet know how to make the results steadier.

Help I need
  1. Check my testing: where might I be fooling myself?
  2. Ideas to improve the edge that don't depend on predicting trades: holding, sizing, costs, or a second strategy.
  3. Advice on what to learn next.

What I found when I tried to predict winners

  • 1Nothing visible at the moment of entry separates future winners from losers (23 features, two periods, live).
  • 2The outcome shows up only through price. After the entry, the shape of the price path adds almost nothing.
  • 3Calendar flows that are supposed to be predictable faded after 2018.
  • 4Machine learning found nothing the simple model missed: out-of-sample AUC 0.528, 0.497 on live trades.
  • 5An unsupervised regime model learned quiet versus busy markets, not trend versus chop.

Test 1

The market at the fill

nothing consistent

I grouped trades by 11 market traits at the fill (time of day, volatility, how far the day had moved, 20-day trend, position in the 12-hour range and more) and 12 features of the entry bar itself (body, wick, where it closed). For each I compared the top third with the bottom third, in live trades and two backtest periods (2017–19 and 2022–26).

One feature pointed the same way in all three samples, and the system already sized trades on it. The rest flipped between periods or stayed inside the noise. Before the fill, winners and losers had travelled the same path: both entered after a run of about 2 ATR in their direction.

Test 2

How fast the market reveals the outcome

only through price

At 15, 30, 60 and 120 minutes after entry I measured how well the trade's current profit or loss predicts whether it ends as a winner (AUC: 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is certain), and whether the shape of the path adds anything.

From the one-hour mark, holding four more hours is worth about zero in every profit-or-loss bucket (between −0.07R and +0.08R), except for trades already above +1R, which keep going (+0.12R and +0.17R in the two periods). After the fill the outcome is built bar by bar. One surprise went against my own idea: for the same profit, bigger bars after entry meant a slightly worse hold.

Test 3

Who has to trade after me?

the footprints faded

Some traders must buy or sell on a timetable: pension funds rebalancing at month end, money arriving at the turn of the month, options dealers hedging into monthly expiry, volatility-target funds after volatility jumps. I checked each for a footprint in the index itself, using daily data from 2012 to 2026.

The month-end effect was real until 2018, then reversed. The monthly options pin was visible intraday in 2017–19 (t −2.18) and disappeared in 2022–26, after options expiring every day took over. Flows with a public timetable get traded ahead of.

Test 4

Can machine learning pick the winners?

no

Meta-labeling: given that the system fired, predict whether the trade ends at +1R or better. Gradient-boosted trees with fixed settings, 8 features from 15-minute bars plus 11 from 1-minute bars (path efficiency, direction flips, tick volume against its usual level, signed volume, distance to VWAP and the opening range). Expanding walk-forward with a 48-hour purge, cross-period tests and live trades.

A faint signal is real (it beats shuffled labels, p = 0.015), but the 1-minute data added nothing, the probabilities were worse than predicting the average, and sizing by the score lowered risk-adjusted returns. A leak check proved the setup could see real information: a feature built from 5 minutes after the fill scored 0.64.

Test 5

Does the market have hidden moods?

activity, not direction

A 3-state hidden Markov model learned market moods from six measurements of each 15-minute bar, without seeing any trades, refitted monthly and read forward only.

Where the edge actually is

Direction at entry is close to a coin flip. The edge is the asymmetry: most trades are cut small, and the few that run carry the whole result. That's why the useful questions are about holding and sizing those trades, about costs, and about adding a second, independent strategy.

How I tested, and the limits

  • Every test was written down before it ran, with its pass or fail rule.
  • A result counts only if it holds in both backtest periods (2017–19 and 2022–26) and points the same way on live trades.
  • Each test includes a planted fake signal, to prove it could have found something.
  • Limits: a demo account with real prices, one market, intraday only. The spread is roughly as large as the edge per trade, and the live record is short and covers several versions of the system.
